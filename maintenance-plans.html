How Care Plans Work

Mix & Match

What typically sits on the calendar.

Use this as a guide when you request a quote. We still walk the property—or review photos—before locking frequencies.

Send Property Details
01

Pressure washing

Driveways, pavers, pool decks, sidewalks, and parking pads. In South Florida, organic growth comes back after rain. Homes often run seasonal or quarterly. High-traffic commercial walks may need monthly service so they never reach the “before” photos.

02

Soft washing

Roofs, stucco, siding, and screen enclosures. These are slower-cycle than glass and faster than paint. Many plans put house or roof washes once or twice a year, with an extra visit after a heavy algae season if needed.

03

Window cleaning

Usually the highest-frequency cleaning service besides lawn work. Storefronts and restaurants often want weekly or biweekly glass. Homes may choose monthly, quarterly, or a seasonal reset after pollen and storms.

04

Landscaping

Mowing, edging, and bed appearance. Peak growing months in this region are weekly; slower periods can drop to biweekly. Landscape is often what keeps the wash looking finished from the street.

05

Painting & coatings

Not a weekly item. Care Plan clients schedule body, trim, or stamped-concrete coating when the surface is due—after a wash—so the project sits on the same relationship instead of starting from zero with a new vendor.

06

Paver sealing & extras

Sealing follows a proper clean. Post-storm blow-off, construction dust, or a one-time elevation can be added to a plan without rewriting the whole calendar.

Why Recurring Here

Florida does not give exteriors a long off-season.

Humidity, rain, irrigation overspray, pollen, and salt air near the coast all shorten how long a one-time wash lasts. A Care Plan is how the property stays in the “after” condition instead of cycling back to neglected between emergency calls.

1

Rainy season

Algae on pavers and decks returns faster. Wash frequency can step up without adding a new contractor.

2

Growing season

Turf and hedges need shorter gaps. Landscape stays weekly while washing stays on its own cadence.

3

Coastal film

Salt and humidity haze glass and metal. Window routes stay tighter than roof or house washes.

4

Storm season

Plans can include a check-in or add-on visit after debris and staining instead of waiting for a new quote from scratch.

5

HOA standards

Appearance rules do not pause. A written rotation is easier to defend than “we’ll call when it looks bad.”
